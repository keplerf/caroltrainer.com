## Workflows

### Content Calendar

Build monthly calendars with:
- Dates, platforms, content pillars, post formats (post/reel/story/article/tweet)
- Topic/angle for each slot with a caption hook (opening line)
- Hashtag suggestions per post
- Monthly theme if provided
- Group by week, one table or section per platform

Save the finished calendar using the Write tool to:
`tools/marketing-planner/output/[month-year]-content-calendar.md`

### Social Media Posts

Platform-specific conventions:

**Instagram**
- Hook: first line must stop the scroll (question, bold statement, or surprising fact)
- Body: 3–8 sentences max, line breaks for readability, emoji sparingly
- CTA: always end with one clear action
- Hashtags: 15–20, mix of niche (#VancouverPersonalTrainer) and broad (#FitnessMotivation)
- Visual brief: describe the ideal photo or reel concept

**Facebook**
- Slightly longer, more conversational than Instagram
- Local Vancouver references encouraged
- 3–5 hashtags max
- Longer-form storytelling works well here

**LinkedIn**
- Professional but personal — Carol as a credible expert, not a salesperson
- Hook: bold insight or counterintuitive statement
- Body: structured with white space, short paragraphs
- Topics: fitness for professionals, workplace wellness, mental health + movement, leadership
- End with a question to invite comments
- 5–8 hashtags (#PersonalTraining #CorporateWellness #VancouverBusiness)
- Optional: suggest this as a LinkedIn article for deeper topics

**Twitter/X**
- Under 280 characters for the main tweet
- Thread format for more complex ideas (number each tweet 1/ 2/ etc.)
- 2–3 hashtags only
- Reframe longer Instagram/LinkedIn content as a punchy insight or tip

### Cross-Platform Adaptation

When adapting content across platforms:
- Start from the core idea, not the original text
- Rewrite natively for each platform — don't just truncate or copy-paste
- Show all adaptations clearly labeled by platform
- Note format differences (e.g. "turn this into a reel concept for Instagram, article for LinkedIn")

### Thought Leadership (LinkedIn)

For building Carol's professional authority:
- **Focus areas**: fitness for busy professionals, corporate wellness ROI, injury prevention at work, mental health through movement
- **Content types**: LinkedIn articles (1,000–1,500 words), newsletters, industry commentary posts
- Provide: positioning statement, 6–10 article topic ideas, 3 full article outlines, speaking/podcast angles, outreach targets
- Save strategy to `tools/marketing-planner/output/thought-leadership-[focus].md`

### Email Campaigns

Structure every email:
- **3 subject line variants** (curiosity, benefit-driven, direct)
- **Preview text** (40–90 chars)
- **Greeting** (warm, first-name style)
- **Body**: scannable with subheadings, under 400 words
- **CTA**: one clear button/link text
- **P.S.**: adds personality and a secondary CTA

Segments: all-subscribers | leads | active-clients | past-clients | corporate-contacts

### Promotions & Campaigns

Every campaign plan should include:
- Campaign name + tagline
- Offer details (what, how much, conditions)
- Timeline with launch date and milestones
- Per-channel messaging and example posts
- Success metrics tied to platform targets
- Step-by-step action checklist

Promotion types: seasonal offer | referral program | new-client special | 30-day challenge | corporate wellness pitch | client advocacy drive

## Tone Reminders

- ✅ "You've got this" / "Real results, real life"
- ✅ Local Vancouver references (e.g. "whether you're commuting from North Van or walking from Yaletown")
- ✅ Direct CTAs: "Book your free consult at caroltrainer.com"
- ❌ Shame-based language ("stop making excuses", "no more laziness")
- ❌ Overpromising ("lose 20 lbs in 30 days")
- ❌ Generic fitness clichés that don't reflect Carol's voice
