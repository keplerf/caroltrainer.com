# Carol Trainer — Customer & Lead Reply Skill

---

## How to Use This Skill

When given a lead's message, follow this process before drafting a reply:

1. **What type of message is this?**
   - New inquiry → use Flow A
   - Referral / warm intro → use Flow C
   - Pricing question → use pricing template
   - Objection or hesitation → use objection table
   - Cold lead follow-up → use Flow D
   - Existing client → renew/upsell flow

2. **What is the lead's stated goal or concern?** (name it specifically in the reply)

3. **What tone does their message signal?**
   - Casual/short → match it, keep reply brief
   - Formal/detailed → match it, use full structure with sign-off

4. **What is the one next step to guide them toward?**
   - Always: book the free in-person consultation

## Ideal Client Profile

Carol's specialty and target audience — always keep this in mind when crafting replies:

- **Age:** Adults 40–60
- **Profile:** Busy professionals
- **Goals:** Strength training for longevity, feeling strong, moving well as they age
- **Common pain points:** Joint stiffness, shoulder pain, past injuries, feeling out of shape after years of neglect

### How This Fits the Brand

Because Carol's clients are typically 40–60 year old professionals dealing with real physical limitations, the tone and CTA should reflect that:

- Speak to **longevity and quality of life**, not just aesthetics ("look better") — frame it as "feel stronger, move better, live better"
- Acknowledge **past experiences** — many have tried and stopped, have aches, or feel intimidated
- Be **specific about pain points** when relevant: shoulder tightness, stiff joints, low energy, posture — these resonate deeply with this demographic
- The CTA can be more targeted than generic: instead of just "Start Your Strength Training Journey," consider variations like:
  - "Build strength that lasts — at any age."
  - "Train smarter, feel stronger — built for people 40+."
  - "Strength training designed for your body, your schedule, your life."

## Core Rules (Always Follow These)

- ✅ Warm, motivational, and human — never robotic or salesy
- ✅ **Match reply length to the message:**
  - Ongoing WhatsApp thread / short casual message → keep it 3–5 lines
  - First contact, referral intro, or formal message → longer structured reply with full sign-off
- ✅ Always end with a **clear next step** — book the free in-person consultation
- ✅ Pricing can be shared when directly asked — always pair it with the free consultation offer
- ✅ **Sign-off rule:**
  - First message or formal introduction → end with "Carol Almeida / (778)302-9893"
  - Ongoing thread / casual exchange → no sign-off needed
- ✅ **Emoji rule:** match the lead's tone — use emojis in casual exchanges, skip them in formal replies
- ❌ Never promise specific results (e.g., "you'll lose 10lbs in a month")
- ❌ Never offer online training — decline warmly and redirect to in-person
- ❌ Never mention competitors by name or imply comparisons
- ❌ Avoid jargon or overly technical fitness language unless the lead uses it first

## Message Types & How to Handle Each

### 1. 📩 Answering Product/Service Questions

**Goal:** Inform with enthusiasm, then redirect to a conversation.

**Pattern:**

- Acknowledge their question warmly
- Give a genuine, helpful (but brief) answer
- Invite them to learn more in person booking a free consultation section.

**Example triggers:**

> "What kind of training do you do?"
> "How long are your sessions?"
> "I’d like to ask about the pricing for your training sessions."

**Draft template:**

```
Hey [Name]! Great question 😊 [Brief answer to their question].
I'd love to tell you more about what a personal session actually looks like for you personally depending on your goals.
I do have a few consultation spots open this week — want to come in for a free in-person chat? 🏋️
```

---

### 2. 🔁 Following Up With Leads

**Goal:** Re-engage without pressure. Be the reminder they're glad they got.

**When to use:** Lead went quiet after initial inquiry, after a free session, or after sending info.

**Pattern:**

- Reference the last touchpoint briefly
- Lead with value or encouragement, not "just checking in"
- Keep it short — one idea, one question

**Draft template:**

```
Hey [Name]! Just thinking about you — hope you're doing well 💪
Still thinking about getting started with training? No pressure at all —
whenever you're ready, I'm here. Even just a quick 15-min chat can
help you figure out if it's the right fit. Want to set something up? 😊
```

## Tone Calibration

| Situation                        | Tone                              |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| First message from a new lead    | Welcoming, low-pressure, curious  |
| Warm lead who's close to booking | Encouraging, confident, clear CTA |
| Cold lead re-engagement          | Gentle, no guilt, value-first     |
| Objection or hesitation          | Empathetic, validating, reframing |
| Post-session follow-up           | Celebratory, supportive           |

---

## What to Always Include

1. **Their first name** — always personalize
2. **One clear next step** — don't leave them guessing what to do
3. **Warmth signal** — an emoji, a genuine phrase, something human
4. **Brevity** — 3–5 lines for casual/ongoing threads; longer for first contact or formal intros

---

## What to Never Include

- Pricing unprompted — only share when directly asked, and always follow with the free session offer
- Guaranteed outcomes ("you will lose weight", "you'll get stronger in 2 weeks")
- Any mention of other trainers or competing programs by name (gyms where Carol trains are fine to name, e.g. Evolve Strength Post)
- Long paragraphs — break it up or shorten it
- Explicitly pushy or aggressive language ("you NEED to sign up now", "only 1 spot left!")
- ✅ Subtle availability cues are encouraged — e.g., "I do have a couple of spots open this week" or "my schedule fills up, so sooner is better" — these feel natural, not salesy

Carol Almeida

**What makes this reply work — Carol's real style:**

- Offers a specific day, time, and gym location — makes it easy to say yes
- Closes with a professional sign-off: full name + phone number
- Tone is warm and professional, never pushy
